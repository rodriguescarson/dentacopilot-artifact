# Evidence cards (paraphrased, open-source style)

[E001] Recall interval after a routine prophylaxis on a healthy adult is
typically six months. A patient with stable periodontal health and low
caries risk will most often return for a periodic oral evaluation (D0120)
followed by another prophylaxis (D1110), with bitewing radiographs
indicated annually for moderate-risk patients and every 18–24 months for
low-risk patients.

[E002] Following a deep posterior caries lesion that involves multiple
surfaces and approaches the pulp, indirect pulp capping with a glass
ionomer or calcium silicate base, followed by a definitive resin composite,
is preferred when the pulp is clinically vital and asymptomatic. If
post-operative symptoms develop, root canal therapy on the affected tooth
becomes the next likely procedure.

[E003] After non-surgical root canal therapy on a posterior tooth, a
full-coverage restoration (typically a crown such as D2740 or D2750) is
recommended to prevent vertical fracture; the most common immediate next
procedure is a core build-up (D2950) followed by the definitive crown.
Anterior teeth may often be restored with a direct composite without a
crown when remaining tooth structure is adequate.

[E004] When an extraction is indicated for a non-restorable tooth and the
patient is a candidate for implant rehabilitation, the next decision point
is extraction with or without immediate implant placement. Bone grafting
(socket preservation) is commonly performed at the time of extraction to
preserve ridge dimensions for delayed implant placement (typically 3–4
months later).

[E005] Periodontal staging follows clinical attachment loss and bone-level
findings. After a clinical diagnosis of periodontitis, scaling and root
planing per quadrant (D4341 for ≥4 teeth, D4342 for 1–3 teeth) is the
standard initial therapy, followed by re-evaluation at 4–6 weeks. After
re-evaluation, periodontal maintenance (D4910) every 3 months replaces
routine prophylaxis.

[E006] In children with active early-childhood caries, silver diamine
fluoride (D1354) can be applied to arrest the carious lesion when
restoration is not immediately feasible. The expected next visit is a
follow-up evaluation (D0120 or D0150) and either retreatment with SDF or
progression to a restorative procedure depending on lesion activity.

[E007] An impacted third molar that is symptomatic, has carious involvement
of the second molar, or is associated with pericoronitis is generally
indicated for surgical removal. Removal codes vary by depth and bony
involvement (D7220 soft-tissue, D7230 partial-bony, D7240 complete-bony,
D7241 with complications). Local anesthesia (D9215) is routinely co-coded.

[E008] Following implant placement (D6010), the next steps are abutment
selection (D6056 prefab or D6057 custom), then crown delivery (D6058 or
D6065). Healing time is typically 3–6 months between placement and the
restorative phase, during which the patient may receive interim hygiene
visits.

[E009] When a patient presents with a fractured cusp on a posterior tooth
that has a large existing restoration but no pulpal symptoms, indirect
restoration (typically a crown after a core build-up) is preferred over
replacing the failing direct restoration. The likely sequence is core
build-up (D2950) followed by a porcelain-fused-to-metal or all-ceramic
crown (D2750 or D2740).

[E010] Comprehensive oral evaluation (D0150) is performed at first visit
or after an extended absence (typically 3+ years). It is followed in the
same visit or shortly after by intraoral imaging (D0210 full-mouth series
or D0274 bitewings, depending on age, risk profile, and time since last
imaging) and, when indicated, prophylaxis (D1110/D1120).

[E011] Fluoride varnish (D1206) is recommended for moderate- and high-risk
caries patients at twice-yearly intervals. The procedure is generally
applied at the same visit as a routine prophylaxis.

[E012] Sealants (D1351) are placed on caries-free permanent molars
(typically the first and second molars in children) shortly after
eruption. The expected next procedure on a sealant tooth is a routine
recall evaluation; sealant retention is checked at subsequent visits.

[E013] Comprehensive orthodontic treatment for adolescents (D8080) and
adults (D8090) is followed by interval adjustment visits (also commonly
billed under the same comprehensive code in many practices) and
post-treatment retention with a removable retainer (D8210) once active
treatment is complete.

[E014] An interim caries-arresting medicament (D1354 SDF) on an early
lesion is followed at the next visit either by reapplication if the lesion
is still active, or by a definitive restoration (D2391 single-surface
posterior composite is most common) once activity is controlled.

[E015] When a tooth has had a previous root canal that has failed
(persistent peri-apical lesion, sinus tract, or symptoms), retreatment
codes apply: D3346 anterior, D3347 premolar, D3348 molar. If retreatment
is contraindicated, apicoectomy (D3410 anterior) or extraction is the
next step.

[E016] In an edentulous patient receiving a complete denture, the most
common procedural sequence is impressions and fitting (one or two visits),
delivery of a maxillary (D5110) or mandibular (D5120) complete denture,
followed by adjustment recalls.

[E017] In a patient with diabetes mellitus, periodontal therapy is more
intensive: scaling and root planing is preferred over prophylaxis when
inflammation is present (D4346 for full-mouth gingival inflammation
without periodontitis), and 3-month periodontal maintenance recall is
usual once periodontitis is established.

[E018] A patient who has had an extraction and is awaiting implant
placement may receive a temporary removable partial denture or a
maintenance evaluation in the interim. The next definitive procedure is
typically the implant placement at 3–4 months post-extraction if a graft
was placed.

[E019] When palliative pain treatment (D9110) is provided at an emergency
visit for a symptomatic tooth, the most common next visit is either an
endodontic procedure on that tooth (RCT or pulpotomy) or, if the tooth is
non-restorable, an extraction.

[E020] An occlusal guard (D9944 hard appliance, full arch) is a typical
follow-up after multiple-tooth restorative work in a patient with
attrition or bruxism. The next likely visits are the delivery /
adjustment of the guard and then a routine recall.
